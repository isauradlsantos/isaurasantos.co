Isaura Santos

There's no "but now you're fine."

A project collecting the stories of people whose lives were forever changed by a diagnosis.

I was diagnosed with breast cancer at 30, in January 2020. Six years later, I can promise you I'm a different person — more afraid of new things, more sensitive around exams and follow-ups. The health anxiety is real, and it's hard.

I want to tell my story. About how going back to your life isn't simple. How there's no "but now you're fine" — not ever again. I thought other people might relate, and music didn't feel like the right medium for this.

But as I started writing it down, I realized this story is much bigger than me. Unfortunately.

The world is optimized for getting things done. And I'm incredibly grateful for all the care I received when I needed it. But I think we can keep evolving — the conversation needs to be bigger, and it can be.

How can we be gentle with people whose lives have been forever changed by illness?

What does it mean to wait days, even weeks, for a call from the hospital to schedule a follow-up — when you're someone who's unlearned how to make simple plans?

What does it mean to still have questions, to be scared of going through treatment again? It doesn't help when people say, "you could get cancer again, just like I could."

People who've had a stroke are changed forever. People who received a transplant and are suddenly expected to feel perfect, to be thriving — after years of rebuilding who they are around their illness — might not be thriving at all.

Anyway — I believe the best way to capture these stories is through conversation.

Who I'm looking for

Anyone whose life was split into a before and an after by illness — and who's still living in the after. You don't need a diagnosis that people recognize, and you don't need to have made peace with it. I'm looking for the honest version, not the inspirational one.

Maybe that's you if:

  • You had cancer and everyone treats you as "cured," but every scan still empties your stomach.
  • You survived a stroke and are quietly relearning parts of who you were.
  • You received a transplant or major surgery and feel guilty that you're not as grateful-and-thriving as people expect.
  • You live with a chronic or autoimmune condition that reshaped your plans, your work, your sense of the future.
  • You went through intensive treatment — ICU, long hospital stays — and came out changed in ways that are hard to explain.
  • You're the person beside someone who went through this, and it changed you too.

If you're reading this and thinking "my story isn't big enough" — that's exactly the kind of story I want to hear.

Share your story

You don't need to tell the whole thing. Even a few lines is enough to start:

  • What happened, and when
  • What changed for you afterward — the part people don't usually ask about
  • Whether you'd be open to a conversation

Write as much or as little as feels right. There's no wrong way to do this.

Our conversations are private. Nothing you share gets used anywhere without your say — I'll only ever share what you feel okay with.

Send your story to isauramusic@gmail.com. I read everything, and I'll reach out after the summer to talk. In Portuguese or English.
